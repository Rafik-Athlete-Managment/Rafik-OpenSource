### Preliminary sports-catalog findings

The shared exercise model ([Prisma schema](../../apps/backend/prisma/schema.prisma)) has category, body-part, target-muscle and generic kind facets; it has no explicit sport, drill phase, direction/change-of-direction, or performance-test semantics. A name scan of the imported SQL corpus found a few potentially useful entries such as `skater hops` and `wind sprints`, but did not find named `shuttle`, `agility` or `cone` drills. `wind sprints` is classified as generic `strength`, despite its running instructions. These are discovery signals, not a complete rights-checked content audit or evidence that the current library meets coach needs. The user-facing picker, equipment filtering, media coverage, safe instructions and source rights still need a focused review before changing shared catalog structure or importing material.

## Required task and authorization map

Before schema or UI work, record each journey as **actor × organization/context × resource × lifecycle action**, with allowed and denied outcomes. At minimum, cover:

- Athlete: join, see assignments, record training, view assessment history, request a correction, review any injury information held about them, and export their own records.
- Coach/trainer: create and deliver training, review assigned performance work, and lose access immediately when unassigned or revoked. Health access must be an explicit policy, not an implied consequence of trainer status.
- Health staff (if approved): access only an explicitly authorized organization roster and the specific health fields needed for their task; record and correct entries with attributable audit history.
- Institution admin: manage organizational membership and operations without automatically receiving sensitive health details. Any emergency or exceptional access needs an explicit, auditable rule.
- Platform operator: operate the self-hosted service without ambient permission to inspect university athlete records.
- One institutional role per membership: administrator, trainer or athlete. The supported web app now uses organization workspaces only; legacy standalone coach screens redirect into the closest organization workflow. See [ADR 0019](../decisions/ADR-0019-single-role-dashboards.md) and [ADR 0021](../decisions/ADR-0021-organization-only-web-experience.md).

Each journey must test direct API access as well as navigation, cross-organization identifiers, former staff, pending command replay, downloads, export, record correction, and audit integrity. UI hiding is not authorization.

## Accepted v1 policies

The founder directed **limited coach access** and a **fixed product-wide minimum** for consent and retention (16 October 2026). A separate health-staff role is not part of the current v1 direction. Closed records expire 12 months after closure.

### Proposed v1 privacy floor

- A coach sees only the assigned athlete's current `modified`/`unavailable` participation status and effective date. Diagnosis, body area, treatment, attachments, narrative and prior history are excluded. Unassigned and former athletes are excluded.
- The athlete reviews, exports, corrects or closes their records. The admin screen exposes consent prerequisites only; platform operators have no product-level record access. Sensitive reads, changes and exports are recorded. Record-linked audit rows are deleted with sensitive content after 12 months; an aggregate instance-level cleanup checkpoint remains.
- The organization records adult/minor classification and verifies guardian authorization for a minor; the athlete must assent before recording status. Only the verifying actor and time are stored, not identity documents or guardian medical data. Withdrawal hides existing status from coaches and blocks new entries. Closed record content and associated audit/history rows are deleted after 12 months.
- Keep v1 entries non-diagnostic and structured. Do not collect free-form clinical notes, diagnoses, treatment plans or files. Make the source of each entry clear and avoid implying that Rafik or a coach has medically assessed the athlete.
- Retention: active entries remain until closed, withdrawn, or the athlete leaves the trainee role. A daily API timer closes entries for inactive/non-trainee members and deletes closed records after 12 months. Startup retries cleanup. Operational monitoring and deployment-offline behavior still need acceptance.

These are product privacy safeguards, not a statement of legal compliance. Minors' representative and access rights depend on applicable law; for example, U.S. HHS guidance defers to state or other law for personal-representative authority ([HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/personal-representatives/index.html)). The UK ICO's children's guidance calls for collecting only data needed for the specific service purpose ([ICO data-minimisation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/8-data-minimisation/)). University privacy/security review remains required before real athlete data is used.

1. **Purpose and authoring:** founder direction is athlete-authored structured participation status, with correction/closure. No diagnosis, note, body area, treatment or attachment.
2. **Fixed retention interval:** founder selected 12 months after closure; code implements the daily purge.
3. **Withdrawal and exports:** founder policy is immediate coach hiding and no new entries after withdrawal. Athletes can export their own structured status/history; export is logged, and the exported file omits organization/member/actor identifiers. Record-linked audit is deleted with the record after 12 months; the non-identifying deployment checkpoint remains.

University review remains required before real athlete data is used. No health values belong in fixtures, analytics, support tickets or existing notes fields.
